The screener's picture of a winner

It averages every founder in the history who exited, then scores new applicants by how closely they resemble that average. This is prototype matching, the same way people judge whether a robin or a penguin is "more of a bird."

How this world works (and what's made up)
  • Talent is equal across groups. Each founder has a hidden potential score drawn the same way for everyone. That's a stated assumption, not a finding.
  • Traction is a noisy signal of potential. Revenue growth tracks potential but with a lot of noise, as early metrics do.
  • Outcomes include a network advantage. Elite-school and hub founders get a boost to exit odds from follow-on access and connections, separate from talent. Loosely motivated by Howell & Nanda (2024) and Gompers et al. on networks; the size is illustrative.
  • History is skewed. The fund's 300 past deals were 12% women, 60% elite school and 75% hub. Illustrative, in the range of reported funding gaps (PitchBook, Carta), not real fund data.
  • The screener is a prototype matcher. It scores applicants by traction plus resemblance to the average past winner (Rosch's prototype theory; Bonelli 2023 finds AI-using VCs drift toward startups resembling past ones).
  • Only funded founders produce outcomes. This is the selective-labels problem (Lakkaraju et al. 2017). Rejected founders never teach the model anything.
  • Nothing you click is saved or sent anywhere.
